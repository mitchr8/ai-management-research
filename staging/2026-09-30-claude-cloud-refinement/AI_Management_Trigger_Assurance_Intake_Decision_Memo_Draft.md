### 4.1 Who detects triggers
FACT: F4, F5, F7, F12. CURRENT RULE: event-driven detection by Chief (sweep) and Delivery (SENT/DONE, Architect NEXT_DUE) for existing trigger classes; no owner for obligations outside those classes; no new Auditor role.
CANDIDATE options (register RAT-017 A–I, every draft's framing preserved): (A) Executive Operations & Delivery Assurance — already owns WAIT_TRIGGER lanes, INV-RESUME and NEXT_DUE tracking; bounded as "operational assurance only" and blind to session-internal triggers; (B) Chief of Staff — owns the sweep and the rendering; "cannot be both the forgetter and the watchdog" and its CURRENT is 71k; (C) Architect — owns the only cadence design; read-only and 7/30-day latency; (D) a shared registry module with split ownership (Architect designs schema; Delivery detects and materialises packets for routed lanes; Chief sweeps and renders; CEO adjudicates) — a function, not a role; still passive; (E) an external clock as executor or notifier, never sole clock; (F) future Agent Relay durable workflows — backlog only; (G) the Efficiency function's wake-up path (Checklist #7 item 5); (H) a dedicated Trigger Assurance function; (I) defer until P2. Draft A observes that "D plus E is the combination the sources' constraints point toward without naming it" [Draft A § 5.1] — recorded as that draft's reading, not a recommendation of this memo.
CANDIDATE reading of the ratified baseline: for existing classes the detector is already named (Chief at activation; Delivery at SENT/DONE and for NEXT_DUE); the open cell is detection for (i) obligations that never entered a mailbox, (ii) session-internal signals (context growth, compaction), and (iii) the missed-sweep case itself. A design can therefore extend the ratified split rather than replace it.
DECISION REQUIRED: RAT-017 (Architect A4 -> CEO -> Owner if a role, automation or cadence is involved).

### 4.2 What the authoritative clock and state should be
FACT: F6, F7. CURRENT RULE: NEXT_DUE semantics for the Architect audit (durable activation state, not proof of execution); AS_OF marks state freshness.
CANDIDATE: separate three things the sources currently conflate — (1) authoritative trigger STATE = a durable registry entry (due/condition, last fired, current state), the only thing any reader trusts; (2) the CLOCK = whoever reads the registry and compares it with now (a Chief or Delivery activation, or an external read-only job) — plural and redundant by design; (3) EXECUTION = the exact packet or the AI role's action, evidenced separately (SENT/DONE). Under this reading "sole authoritative clock" is avoided because the state lives in Drive and any reader can detect overdue; an external job adds a reader, not a truth. The Architect audit already works this way (NEXT_DUE in Architecture Current; Delivery and Chief read it). Generalising the AUDIT_AS_OF reset pattern to every recurring trigger is the CANDIDATE Draft B tags for Item #8 [Draft B Diagram 4(e), "CANDIDATE — generalizing the Architect's AUDIT_AS_OF reset pattern"].
DECISION REQUIRED: RAT-017 (sub-question: is a durable registry entry the authoritative state, with readers as redundant clocks); RAT-018 for which external reader, if any.

### 4.3 Role of automations versus passive chats
FACT: F1 and F2 limit external jobs to executor/notifier; the Brain limits authorised automation to an Owner-notification surface; the Owner reserved reminder-slot and tool-cost constraints to Item #8; reminder-slot explosion is warned against (F3 item 9).
CANDIDATE options (RAT-018 A–E): (A) ChatGPT scheduled task or reminder slot; (B) Agent Relay durable recurring job — "Backlog only, post-stabilization"; (C) external cloud scheduled job (Claude/Codex) as a read-only notifier that measures and writes one scan file, never executes [Draft E EFF-011]; (D) status quo — the Owner opening a Chief or Delivery chat is the clock; (E) executing automation — conflicts with the Brain's notification-only rule as written. Draft E's EFF-011 design is the most specific candidate: a read-only sweep that records sizes, AS_OF staleness, unlabelled closures, fired triggers and Architect NEXT_DUE into one scan file and notifies.
CANDIDATE boundary that every option shares: an automation may add a LAST_CHECKED stamp and a notification; it may not transition a lane, write another owner's surface, or count as evidence that a chat executed anything (Brain rule above).
DECISION REQUIRED: RAT-018 (Architect -> CEO -> Owner, because tool, subscription, reminder-slot and runtime costs are Owner-weighed).

### 4.4 Missed-run detection
FACT: F7 — impossible today; F3 item 8 requires catch-up detectability. CURRENT RULE: none beyond the Architect's NEXT_DUE.
CANDIDATE mechanism (minimal): every registry entry and the registry itself carry LAST_CHECKED (when any reader last evaluated it) separate from AS_OF (when its state last changed) and from LAST_FIRED; each recurring entry carries EXPECTED_CADENCE; at any activation the reader computes overdue = now − LAST_CHECKED > EXPECTED_CADENCE, runs the sweep first, records MISSED_RUN_COUNT and the gap, and only then proceeds. The Owner sees a missed run only when it crossed an Owner-friction threshold (4.9). This needs exactly one new field class (LAST_CHECKED) on one surface; it does not need a clock.
DECISION REQUIRED: RAT-017 (sub-question: adopt LAST_CHECKED and a catch-up rule as part of the registry schema); the cadence value itself stays with Item #8 per the Owner.

### 4.5 Central registry / watchdog design
FACT: F4 (five homes), F11 (four sweep enumerations), F5 (P2 lists a "central Trigger Assurance registry/watchdog model"). CURRENT RULE: "One owner briefing surface"; the Owner Notifications file is the ratified Owner-facing follow-up surface; TRIGGER_BOUND items live in project inboxes; WAIT_TRIGGER lanes live in Delivery state.
CANDIDATE options: (R-A) one central registry that indexes every due obligation by stable ID and points to its home surface (lanes stay in Delivery state, TRIGGER_BOUND items stay in project inboxes, Owner Notifications becomes either the registry's Owner-facing view or is absorbed) [Draft B R6 registry A; Draft A § 5.1 option D; Draft C M-PHASE]; (R-B) keep the four or five existing homes and ratify only a sweep-order rule and the LAST_CHECKED stamp [Draft B R6 registry B]; (R-C) fold intake into the registry so one surface serves intake and triggers [Draft B R2 option C].
CANDIDATE distinction this memo adds: a registry is state about obligations (due, owner, suppression, close condition); an event ledger (RAT-008) is history about events (SENT, DONE, transitions). The drafts never merge them, and a merged file would recreate the Chief-handoff transcript problem (F2 of the report). A registry entry should point to ledger evidence, not contain it. Likewise the phase/hold register (RAT-009) is the subset of registry entries whose trigger is a decision, so a registry absorbs RAT-009 if created.
CANDIDATE size discipline: the registry is an operational CURRENT surface under the ratified 12k/15k/25k thresholds; closed entries archive to an index. A registry that grows is the failure it was meant to prevent.
DECISION REQUIRED: RAT-017 (registry yes/no and form); RAT-009 (absorbed or separate); RAT-008 stays a separate decision.

### 4.10 Minimum schema (CANDIDATE)

### 4.11 Failure modes the design must name (CANDIDATE list; each traces to evidence)
1. Silent miss — nobody is activated, so no reader exists (F6, F9). Mitigation candidates: redundant readers; LAST_CHECKED; one recurring sweep.
2. Stale registry — AS_OF is fresh but LAST_CHECKED is old (F7). Mitigation: the two stamps are distinct.
3. Duplicate wake — two homes hold the same obligation (MGMT-NOTIF-001 in the file and in Delivery's index; F4, F8). Mitigation: one authoritative home per entry with a pointer from the other (RAT-035).
4. False wake — a fired item already consumed or superseded (F14). Mitigation: evidence-first before any packet (ratified).
5. Registry bloat — the registry becomes a transcript (the Chief-handoff pattern). Mitigation: CURRENT thresholds apply; closed entries archive.
6. Reminder-slot explosion — per-project scheduled reminders (F3 item 9). Mitigation: one sweep, one briefing mechanism (ratified escalation rule).
7. Executor skipped — a scheduled job did not run because of provider occupancy (F1, Design Brief § 8). Mitigation: job is reader/notifier, never sole clock; MISSED_RUN visible at next activation.
8. Misroute — a packet reaches the wrong chat (GO10 -> ULTUSB class; identity gate ratified, enforcement open, RAT-026/027). Mitigation: EXECUTOR binding uses the identity gate once it exists.
9. Registry/ledger drift — two truths for one event (CEO-OPS-010 on six surfaces). Mitigation: registry points to ledger evidence; RAT-008/030 decide the writer.
10. Over-centralisation — the watchdog starts deciding instead of noticing. Mitigation: detector, executor and authority are different fields; the registry has no decision rights (§ 4.12).
11. Owner-ping regression — automatic detection generating more Owner packets, not fewer (F10). Mitigation: OWNER_FRICTION_CLASS defaults to NONE; one-task rendering; A-band order.
12. Unverifiable obligations — items whose close condition depends on project files Management does not read (F8, MGMT-NOTIF-001). Mitigation: CLOSE_RESET must name an evidence source inside the Management read set, or the entry is marked UNVERIFIABLE and routed to the owning PM.

### 4.12 Privacy and authority boundaries (CURRENT RULE applied; CANDIDATE wording)
- The registry holds abstract metadata and pointers, never copied sensitive content; for whole-life domains the entry carries the A-band and Owner-action class only (Brain privacy rule; RAT-022 for anything richer).
- The registry is activation state, not proof of execution, and not a mailbox: it cannot be written by project chats as an inbox (that is the intake surface, RAT-025) and it does not replace To_Management for FULL_MANAGED projects (F3 item 3).
- Detector, executor and authority are separate fields; noticing confers no right to decide or to act outside the detector's own scope; the CEO adjudicates cross-role conflicts; the Owner's reserved decisions stay Owner-only.
- No cross-project write: entries about a project point into that project's Management interface; Management does not write project truth (cross-project write firewall, Global Brain).
- No implementation authority is created: the registry schema, if ratified, is a Management standard under the Architect's design and CEO ratification path; nothing in it touches Agent Relay, ULTUSB or GO10 runtimes.

### 4.13 How to avoid turning this into a new executive role
FACT: the five-part graduation threshold (Draft A § 5.0) and the Marketing-pilot template; the responsibility is split today and the measured failure count is one (F-150). CURRENT RULE: no new Auditor; no new executive role without a demonstrated durable responsibility; "Use existing Chief/Delivery routing. Do not create a new role".
CANDIDATE form that stays below the threshold: Trigger Assurance as a procedure and a surface, not a chat — the Architect writes the schema and the sweep-order rule (design); Delivery stamps LAST_CHECKED and materialises packets at SENT/DONE and for lanes it already owns (operational assurance, within its charter); the Chief runs the sweep at every activation and renders (its ratified job); an external read-only job, if the Owner authorises one, adds a reader and a notification; the CEO adjudicates. Graduation to a function with its own slot, or later a role, only on measured evidence: missed-trigger rate, catch-up latency and false-wake count over a defined period (the candidate evaluation criteria in Draft A § 1.12), reviewed under the Marketing-pilot exit pattern. The three "W?" ownership cells in Draft A's matrix stay unwritten until RAT-017 names the owner.
